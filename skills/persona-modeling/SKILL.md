# Persona Modeling

The profile is static but the user edits it over time. When applying a concept, do
**not** try to address the whole profile at once. Select the ONE goal or challenge
where *this specific concept's mechanism* gives the most leverage, and build the
scenario there.

## How to choose the anchor

- **Prefer challenges with an interpersonal or power dynamic** — someone controls
  a resource, credit, access, or a decision, or there's a recurring relational
  pattern. That is where these concepts actually bite. A concept about superiors →
  the chair/committee challenge; a concept about reputation → the
  visibility/citation challenge.
- **Prefer concrete, near-term situations** over abstract ambitions ("the grant
  meeting next week" over "advance my career").
- **Match the concept to the friction.** Don't force every concept onto the same
  challenge; rotate to whichever one this mechanism illuminates best.
- **Respect the stated tone preference** in the profile.

## Honor their world (locale is not optional)

- **Use the person's country and institutional system as given.** If they work in
  Indian academia, the scenario must run on *that* system — UGC/NAAC, DST/SERB/ICSSR
  funding, HoD/Dean hierarchy, CAS/API promotions, seniority and reservation
  dynamics — never a generic US/Western "tenure committee" or "grant office."
- Name the real bodies, roles, and rituals of their world. Specificity to *their*
  system is what makes the advice feel written for them.

## Boundaries

- You may invent a plausible *situation* consistent with the person's domain.
- You may **not** invent *facts about the person* beyond what the profile supports
  (no fictional colleagues by name, no events they didn't mention as types).
- If the profile is thin, stay closer to the domain in general terms rather than
  fabricating specifics.
