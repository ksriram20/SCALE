# The bar: flat vs. crafted (same facts, same honesty)

## FLAT — what NOT to ship (a greeting, not a hook)

> "Welcome back to your briefing, Sriram. Today we work through Life Games from
> Eric Berne's Games People Play: Alcoholic, Debtor, Kick Me. For each one we'll get
> the game exactly as Berne described it, then put it to work in your world. Let's begin."

True and personal — but it opens like a meeting agenda. No pull.

## CRAFTED — magnetic delivery, nothing invented

> "There's a pattern running through your hardest weeks, Sriram — the committee that
> never ends, the senior who always needs one more favour. You've felt it. You've
> never named it. Eric Berne named it sixty years ago, and the moment you can name
> one of these games, it starts to lose its grip on you. [[pause:1.4]] We'll take
> five. Each one exactly as Berne defined it — then exactly where it's running in
> your week. The goal isn't to get cynical about people. It's to stop being played."

Why it works: the author is still credited honestly (**Berne**), not a word of the
content changed, the listener's real world is named, identity framing ("you've felt
it… you've never named it") pulls them in, and it ends on a reframe — *not cynical,
just clear-eyed*. No padding, no misattribution. That is the whole move: their craft,
our honesty.

## Close — flat vs. crafted

FLAT: "That is your briefing. Pick the one that fit best, and use it this week."

CRAFTED: "Five games. One thread: the people around you are running scripts, and you
can finally read them. Pick the one you saw yourself in just now — and the next time
it starts, name it, even just to yourself. That's where the grip breaks."
