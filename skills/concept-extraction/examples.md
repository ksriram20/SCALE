# Worked examples: faithful vs. drifted extraction

These illustrate the *bar*, not the content of your unit. They are drawn from a
different book (Robert Greene's *48 Laws of Power*) purely to show faithful-vs-
drifted extraction — do not import their content into your answer.

## GOOD — faithful, cited, keeps the edge

Source excerpt (Law 1): "...Always make those above you feel comfortably
superior. In your desire to please and impress them, do not go too far in
displaying your talents or you might accomplish the opposite — inspire fear and
insecurity."

```
  "title": "Never Outshine the Master",

The danger is not lack of ability but its conspicuous display: outshine a master and you do not earn admiration, you 'inspire fear and insecurity'. The move is to let superiors take the credit and appear more brilliant than they are, so your competence reassures rather than threatens them.",
  "quotes": ["make those above you feel comfortably superior", "inspire fear and insecurity"]
}
```

Why it passes: the mechanism names the *insecurity-of-superiors* engine, every
claim traces to the excerpt, quotes are verbatim, the cynical edge survives.

## BAD — drifted into self-help (REJECT this pattern)

```
{
  "title": "Never Outshine the Master",
  "mechanism": "Stay humble and lift others up.",
  "explanation": "This law teaches the value of humility and teamwork. By being modest, you build trust and create a positive environment where everyone succeeds together.",
  "quotes": []
}
```

Why it fails: "humility and teamwork" is the *comfortable* version, not Greene's.
The real point is strategic management of a superior's ego for self-protection,
not virtue. No quotes. This is exactly the misrepresentation we exist to avoid.
