# Worked examples: the bar (stance = "both")

Illustrative only — shows the *shape* of a good "both" application, not the content
of your unit. Persona is the same academic profile.

## GOOD — recognize, then hand over the decision

Illustrative concept: a game whose mechanism is "one person endlessly solicits
advice only to reject every suggestion, to prove no one can help and stay in
control." (A stand-in — not your unit.)

> The engine here is simple: the point isn't to solve the problem, it's to defeat
> every solution, because being un-helpable is the payoff. Watch where this shows
> up for you. In your research group, a senior collaborator keeps bringing the
> same stalled analysis to every meeting, asking what to do, then batting down
> each idea you offer — "tried that," "won't work here." You leave drained, the
> analysis never moves, and somehow you feel vaguely at fault. That's the tell:
> the yes-but reflex, and the quiet sense that you failed to help.
>
> So you've got two ways to play it. The clean move is to step out of the game
> entirely: stop supplying solutions. Reflect it back instead — "you've ruled out
> everything I'd suggest; what are you going to try?" The game needs a rescuer; if
> you won't audition, it collapses, and you get your time back. That's the honest
> play, and usually the right one.
>
> Or, if you need this person's goodwill before next month's authorship talk, you
> can let them win the round on purpose — offer a deliberately weak idea, let them
> knock it down, let them feel clever and in control. It buys you standing. But be
> clear-eyed: you've fed the game, and they'll bring it back. That's the cost. Your
> call which one is worth it this week.

Why it passes: every move depends on the *reject-every-solution* mechanism (the
rescuer-bait, the yes-but tell). It names the scene in the persona's world, gives
the defuse play AND the tactical play with its cost, and leaves the choice to them.

## BAD — generic advice wearing the concept's hat (REJECT)

> Set boundaries with difficult colleagues and communicate clearly. Stay confident,
> don't take it personally, and focus on your own work. With a positive attitude
> you'll build better relationships over time.

Why it fails: "set boundaries, stay positive, communicate clearly" would work under
*any* concept or none. It never touches the mechanism. This is the failure mode the
whole product exists to beat.
